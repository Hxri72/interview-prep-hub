## 💡 What they really want to know

The interviewer is asking:
- Will you **stay and grow** here?
- Do your goals **match this role**?
- Do you have a **plan**, or are you drifting?

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **school cricket player**.

He says, "In three years, I want to open the batting for the district team, and help the junior players." That's clear, ambitious and realistic. It also helps the coach — a stronger batter and a helper for the juniors.

- **Opening the batting** = owning bigger systems.
- **District team** = a realistic next level, not "World Cup tomorrow".
- **Helping juniors** = mentoring.
- **Helping the coach** = your growth also helps the company.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Skills** you want to be strong in.
2. **Ownership** — what you want to be responsible for.
3. **Helping others** — mentoring, reviews.
4. **Link to this role** — why this job is the right next step.

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "In three years, I see myself as a strong backend-focused full stack engineer who owns systems end to end — from design to production.
>
> I want to go deeper into system design and scaling, and keep building AI features. I enjoyed building the AI voice agent, and I want to do more work like that, where AI solves a real problem for users.
>
> I'd also like to mentor junior developers and help with code reviews, because explaining things also makes me better.
>
> This role fits that path because [FILL IN: something specific about this company or role — e.g. the scale of the product, the backend ownership, or AI work]. I want to grow here while adding real value to the team."

## ✅ Do

- Talk about **skills and ownership**.
- Mention **mentoring** — it shows maturity.
- **Link it to this company** with one specific reason.
- Sound **ambitious but realistic**.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't say "in your position" as a joke.
- Don't say "at a bigger company" or "running my own startup".
- Don't say "I don't know".
- Don't name a very senior title that sounds unrealistic.
