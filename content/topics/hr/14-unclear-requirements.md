## 💡 What they really want to know

Real tasks are often unclear. Interviewers know this.

They want to see that you:
- **Ask questions** instead of guessing.
- **Write things down** so everyone agrees.
- **Check early** so you don't waste days.
- **Work well with product and design.**

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **tailor making a shirt**.

The customer says, "Make me a nice shirt." That's unclear. A good tailor asks: what size? What colour? Full or half sleeves? For a wedding or for the office? Then the tailor writes it down, and does a trial fitting before finishing.

- **"Make me a nice shirt"** = the unclear requirement.
- **Size, colour, sleeves** = your questions.
- **Writing it down** = writing your assumptions in the ticket.
- **The trial fitting** = showing a small first version for feedback.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Read first.** Check the ticket, designs, and any old similar feature.
2. **List questions.** Who uses it? What problem does it solve? What does "done" mean? What about edge cases?
3. **Ask the right person.** Usually the product owner or designer.
4. **Write down assumptions.** Put them in the ticket or a message, so they can be corrected.
5. **Build small, show early.** Get feedback on a first version before finishing.

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "When requirements are unclear, I don't start coding on a guess. First, I read the ticket and look at any designs or similar features we already have.
>
> Then I write a short list of questions — who will use it, what problem it solves, what 'done' looks like, and the edge cases. I take those to the product owner or designer.
>
> If some answers aren't ready yet, I write down my assumptions in the ticket and ask them to confirm. That way nobody is surprised later.
>
> Then I build a small first version and show it early, so we can correct the direction before I spend too much time.
>

## ✅ Do

- Show you **ask questions early**.
- Mention **writing down assumptions**.
- Mention **showing a small version early**.
- Mention working **with product and design**.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't say "I just build what I think is right."
- Don't say "I wait until everything is perfectly clear." Work must still move.
- Don't blame product for unclear tickets.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### What if the product owner is busy and can't answer?

I write my assumptions down, share them in the team channel or ticket, and start with the parts that are clear. I avoid building the unclear part until someone confirms.

### What if requirements change halfway?

I check how much work changes, tell my lead about the new effort, and update the plan. Building in small pieces makes changes cheaper.

### How do you know when a requirement is "clear enough"?

When I can explain who uses it, what they do step by step, and how we'll test that it works.
