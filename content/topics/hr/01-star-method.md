## 💡 What they really want to know

Behavioural questions start with "Tell me about a time when…".

The interviewer wants proof, not opinions. They check three things:
- Can you explain a problem clearly?
- What did **you** do, step by step?
- Did it work, and what did you learn?

STAR is a simple pattern to answer this well.

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of telling your friend about a **cricket match**.

- **Situation:** "Last over, we needed 12 runs."
- **Task:** "I was the batsman on strike."
- **Action:** "I watched the bowler, waited for a full ball, and hit two sixes."
- **Result:** "We won by one wicket."

Your friend understood the whole story in 20 seconds. That is STAR.

- The **match situation** = the Situation.
- **Your job in that moment** = the Task.
- **What you did** = the Action.
- **The score at the end** = the Result.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

| Part | What to say | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Situation** | Where, when, the background. One or two lines. | ~10 sec |
| **T — Task** | Your responsibility or the goal. | ~10 sec |
| **A — Action** | The steps **you** took and why. Most of the answer. | ~45–60 sec |
| **R — Result** | The outcome. A real number if you have one. One thing you learned. | ~15 sec |

Tips:
- Keep the whole answer to **1–2 minutes**.
- Say "I", not "we", for your own work.
- Pick stories from real work. Your resume pages are a good source.

## 🗣️ Sample answer

**Question:** "Tell me about a time you tested something that was hard to test."

> "**Situation:** At SkillKeepr, our product has subscriptions through Stripe. Renewals happen once a month or once a year, so they are hard to test.
>
> **Task:** I worked on the auto-renewal part — the renewal cron job and the webhook handling.
>
> **Action:** Waiting a month for a real renewal was not possible. So I used Stripe's Test Clocks. A Test Clock lets you move time forward in test mode. I created test customers, moved the clock forward, and watched the renewal webhooks arrive. I checked that our system updated the plan correctly. I also tested a failed payment the same way. [FILL IN: one specific thing you found or fixed while testing.]
>
> **Result:** I could test months of renewals in minutes, before going live. [FILL IN: the real result — for example, no renewal issues after release.]"

## ✅ Do

- Prepare 5–6 stories before the interview. Write them down.
- Keep the Situation and Task short.
- Explain **why** you chose each action.
- End with a clear result and one lesson.
- Practise out loud, with a timer.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't spend a whole minute on background.
- Don't say only "we did…". The interviewer can't hire your team.
- Don't invent numbers or results.
- Don't pick a story where you were not really involved.
- Don't criticise teammates or your company in the story.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### What would you do differently next time?

Pick one honest improvement. For example, "I would add automated tests for the webhook earlier." It shows you learn from work.

### What was your exact role, and what did others do?

Be specific. Say what you built and what teammates built. Interviewers check ownership claims.

### How did you measure the result?

Say how you know it worked: logs, tests, user feedback, fewer bugs. If there was no measurement, say so honestly.

### What was the hardest part?

Name one real difficulty and how you got past it. This often becomes a new mini STAR answer.
