## 💡 What they really want to know

At the end, they ask: **"Do you have any questions for us?"**

This tells them:
- Are you **really interested** in this job?
- Did you **think about** the role?
- Are you **curious** — a good sign for an engineer?

It is also **your chance** to check if you want this job.

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of **visiting a new school before joining**.

A smart student asks: "What subjects do you teach well? What do students do after class? How do teachers help if I'm stuck?" Those questions show interest. They also help the student choose the right school.

- **The new school** = the company.
- **The student's questions** = your questions.
- **Choosing the right school** = deciding if this job fits you.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. Prepare **4–5 questions**. Ask **2–3** that weren't already answered.
2. Mix them: **the team**, **the work**, **success in the role**.
3. **Listen** to the answer, and add a short follow-up or comment.
4. **Thank them** at the end.

Good questions to choose from:

| About | Question |
|---|---|
| The team | "How big is the team, and how is work split between frontend and backend?" |
| The tech | "What does your current tech stack look like, and what are you planning to change?" |
| The process | "How does a feature go from an idea to production here?" |
| Quality | "How do you do code reviews and testing?" |
| Success | "What would success look like for this role in the first six months?" |
| Challenges | "What is the biggest technical challenge the team is facing right now?" |
| Growth | "How do engineers learn and grow here?" |
| AI | "Are you using AI tools or building AI features in the product?" |

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "Yes, I have a few questions.
>
> First, how does a feature usually go from an idea to production here — who's involved, and how do you review and test it?
>
> Second, what would success look like for this role in the first six months?
>
> And I'm curious — what's the biggest technical challenge the team is working on right now?"

Then listen, and react briefly — for example: "That sounds interesting. I worked on something similar with [FILL IN: a related project of yours, if it fits]."

## ✅ Do

- Have questions **ready before** the interview.
- Ask about the **work, the team and success**.
- **Listen** and react to the answers.
- Ask one question that **connects to your experience**.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't say "No, I don't have any questions."
- Don't ask things that are on their website, like "What does your company do?"
- Don't start with salary, leave or perks in a technical round.
- Don't ask ten questions when time is short.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### When should I ask about salary?

In the HR round, or when they bring it up. See [salary expectations](topic:hr/salary).

### What if all my questions were already answered?

Say so politely, then ask one about the next steps: "You've answered most of my questions. What are the next steps in the process?"

### Can I ask the interviewer about their own experience?

Yes. "What do you enjoy most about working here?" is a friendly, good question.

### Should I write my questions down?

Yes. It's fine to have a small note in front of you. It shows preparation.
