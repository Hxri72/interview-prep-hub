## 💡 What they really want to know

The interviewer wants to know:
- Do you understand what you are good at?
- Do those strengths match this job?
- Can you prove them with real examples?

A strength without an example is just a claim.

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **shopkeeper selling a phone**.

A weak seller says, "It's a very good phone." A strong seller says, "The battery lasts two days — here, see the test result."

- **"It's a very good phone"** = "I'm hard-working."
- **"The battery lasts two days"** = a specific strength.
- **"See the test result"** = your real work example.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

For each strength (2–3 total):
1. **Name it** in a few words.
2. **Prove it** with a short example — one or two sentences.
3. **Link it** to how it helps this job.

Strengths you can prove from your work — use only the ones that feel true to you:

| Strength | Proof from your work |
|---|---|
| Learning new tech quickly | Built the AI voice agent with Twilio, Google speech services and Claude, and added Grok as a premium voice. [FILL IN: confirm these were new to you] |
| Full stack range | React and TypeScript on the frontend; Node.js, MongoDB and integrations on the backend |
| Careful testing | Tested Stripe auto-renewal with Test Clocks; wrote Jest unit tests |
| Working with third-party APIs | Stripe, unified.to (Greenhouse, Lever, Zoho), OpenAI, Twilio |

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "I'd say my main strength is learning new technology quickly and then shipping something real with it. Recently I built an AI voice agent that calls candidates automatically. It uses Twilio for the calls, Google speech-to-text and text-to-speech, and Claude to drive the conversation. Later I added xAI Grok as a premium voice option. [FILL IN: how quickly you learned it, or what was new to you.]
>
> My second strength is that I'm careful about testing tricky flows. When I worked on Stripe auto-renewal, renewals only happen monthly. So I used Stripe Test Clocks to simulate months passing in test mode, and checked every webhook before release.
>
> I think both are useful here, because [FILL IN: link to their job — e.g. you're building AI features and integrating many services]."

## ✅ Do

- Choose strengths the job description asks for.
- Give one real, short example for each.
- Sound confident but humble.
- Be ready for "tell me more about that example".
- Prepare a different order for different jobs.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't list ten strengths.
- Don't use clichés with no proof: "hard-working", "team player", "perfectionist".
- Don't claim a strength you can't back up.
- Don't exaggerate numbers. The voice agent time saving is an estimate.
- Don't sound arrogant ("I'm the best developer on my team").

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### Can you give another example of that strength?

Have a second example ready for each strength. For testing: Jest unit tests on the backend. [FILL IN: one more real example.]

### What would your manager say your strength is?

[FILL IN: what your manager has actually praised you for.] If you don't know, say what you think and why.

### How has this strength helped your team?

Explain the team result — for example, a feature shipped without bugs, or a new capability added for recruiters.

### Which strength do you want to develop more?

Pick one that links to the job — for example, system design at scale.
