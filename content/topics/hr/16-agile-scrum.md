## 💡 What they really want to know

Your resume says you worked in an **Agile (Scrum)** team.

The interviewer wants to check:
- Do you really know how a sprint works?
- Can you describe **your team's real routine**?
- Do you **communicate** well — updates, blockers, demos?

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **school sports day being organised in weekly steps**.

Every Monday, the class plans what to finish that week. Every morning, each student says what they did yesterday and if they're stuck. On Friday, they show the teacher what's ready. Then they talk about what to do better next week.

- **Monday plan** = sprint planning.
- **Morning check-in** = daily stand-up.
- **Friday show** = sprint review (demo).
- **"What to do better"** = retrospective.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Sprint length** and the main tool you use.
2. **The meetings**: planning, stand-up, review, retro — one line each.
3. **How work is picked and estimated.**
4. **Your part**: how you update the team, raise blockers and ship.
5. **One thing you like** or learned from working this way.

Fill in your team's real details. Don't guess them.

>
> At the start of each sprint we do planning. We pick tasks from the backlog and estimate them with [FILL IN: story points or hours]. Tasks are assigned by [FILL IN: how — e.g. the lead, or people pick them].
>
> Every day we have a short stand-up. I share what I finished, what I'm doing next, and any blockers — for example, if I'm waiting for an API detail from another developer.
>
> At the end of the sprint we demo the work in a review, and then we have a retrospective to discuss what to improve. [FILL IN: one real change your team made after a retro, if you have one.]
>
> I work across the frontend and backend, and I make sure my tasks go through code review and testing before they're marked done."

## ✅ Do

- Give **real details**: sprint length, tool, estimation style.
- Explain **your own part** in each meeting.
- Mention **raising blockers early**.
- Mention **code review and testing** before "done".

## ❌ Don't

- Don't just recite the Scrum guide.
- Don't make up numbers or tools.
- Don't say "the meetings are a waste of time".

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### What do you do if you can't finish a task in the sprint?

I tell the team early, in stand-up, not on the last day. Then we either split the task, move part of it to the next sprint, or get help.

### How do you estimate a task?

I break it into small steps, think about testing and review time, and compare it with similar past tasks. [FILL IN: your team's estimation style.]
