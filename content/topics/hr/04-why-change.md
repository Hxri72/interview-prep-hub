## 💡 What they really want to know

The interviewer wants to check three things:
- Are you leaving **for** something good, or running **from** a problem?
- Will you leave this company quickly too?
- Do you speak respectfully about past employers?

A calm, positive, growth-focused answer passes all three.

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **student moving from a good school to a bigger college**.

The school was good. The student learned a lot there. But now they want harder subjects and new labs.

- **The good school** = SkillKeepr, where you learned and grew.
- **Harder subjects and new labs** = bigger problems and more ownership.
- **Saying thank you to the school** = speaking well of your current company.

Nobody thinks badly of a student for moving on to college.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Appreciate:** one line on what you gained at your current job.
2. **Reason:** one or two growth reasons.
3. **Connect:** why this company fits that reason.

Example reasons — use only the ones that are true for you:
- Work at a bigger scale or with more users.
- Own larger features or services end to end.
- Go deeper into backend, system design, or AI features.
- Work in a bigger team with strong engineering practices.

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "I've learned a lot at SkillKeepr over the last three years. I've worked across the stack — from Stripe auto-renewal and an ATS integration to, most recently, an AI voice agent that calls candidates. I'm grateful for that.
>
> Now I want to take the next step. I'm looking for [FILL IN: your real reasons — e.g. bigger scale, deeper ownership of backend services, more AI work].
>
> From what I've read about [FILL IN: company], you're working on [FILL IN: something specific about them]. That matches what I want to grow into, so this role feels like the right next step."

## ✅ Do

- Lead with something positive about your current job.
- Give one or two clear growth reasons.
- Research the company and mention something specific.
- Keep the same reason in every round of interviews.
- Keep it short and move on confidently.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't criticise your manager, team, salary or company — even if asked directly.
- Don't share internal company problems.
- Don't give five different reasons.
- Don't say "I just want more money" as your main reason.
- Don't sound like you are desperate to leave.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### Is there anything you don't like about your current job?

Stay positive. Talk about something you want **more of**, not something bad. For example: "I'd like to work on systems with more scale." [FILL IN: your honest, positive version.]

### Have you discussed growth with your current manager?

[FILL IN: if true, a short positive line.] If you haven't, say you want a role where this kind of work is the main focus.

### What will make you stay at our company for a long time?

Name what you value: learning, ownership, a good team, interesting problems. Link it to what they offer.

### Do you have other offers?

Be honest and calm. "I'm in conversations with a few companies, but this role is a strong fit because…" [FILL IN: your real situation.]
