## 💡 What they really want to know

Teams disagree all the time. That is normal and healthy.

The interviewer wants to know:
- Can you **share your view with reasons**, not just feelings?
- Do you **listen** to the other side?
- Can you **agree on a decision** and move on, even if it isn't yours?
- Do you **stay friendly** afterwards?

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of **two friends planning a school project**.

One wants a poster. The other wants a slideshow. They each explain why. They look at what the teacher asked for. They pick the slideshow, and they both work hard on it.

- **Poster vs slideshow** = two technical approaches.
- **Explaining why** = giving reasons, not just "I like mine".
- **Checking the teacher's rules** = checking the real requirements or data.
- **Both working hard after** = supporting the final decision.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Situation** — what you were building, and what you disagreed about.
2. **Your view** — what you suggested and **why** (cost, time, risk, users).
3. **Listening** — what their reasons were. Show you understood them.
4. **Deciding** — how you agreed: data, a quick test, the lead deciding, or a middle way.
5. **Result** — what happened, and that the relationship stayed good.

## ✅ Do

- Talk about a **work** topic, like a design or a priority.
- Give **your reasons** clearly.
- Show you **listened** and understood their side.
- Show the **decision** and that you supported it.
- End on a **good working relationship**.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't say bad things about the other person.
- Don't tell a story where you "won" and they "lost".
- Don't pick a big personal fight or an HR issue.
- Don't say "I never disagree with anyone." It sounds like you avoid hard talks.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### What if your lead had decided against you?

I'd say my concerns clearly once, with reasons. If the lead still decides the other way, I commit to it fully and help make it work.

### What would you do if the disagreement kept going?

I'd suggest a small test or look at data. If we still can't agree, I'd ask the lead or a senior to decide, so the work doesn't stop.

### How do you give feedback to a teammate?

Privately, politely, about the code or the plan — not the person. I suggest a change and explain why.
