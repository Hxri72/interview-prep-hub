## 💡 What they really want to know

The interviewer is not trying to catch you out. They want to see:
- Are you honest and self-aware?
- Do you take action to improve?
- Is the weakness a big risk for this job?

An honest weakness with a real plan shows maturity.

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **cricketer who is weak against spin bowling**.

A good player doesn't hide it. They say, "I struggle against spin, so I practise 30 minutes against spinners every day. My average against spin has improved."

- **Weak against spin** = your real weakness.
- **30 minutes of practice daily** = your concrete action.
- **Improved average** = your progress so far.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **Name it:** one real weakness, in a few words.
2. **Example:** when it showed up — keep it small.
3. **Action:** what you are doing about it, specifically.
4. **Progress:** what has improved so far.

Possible weaknesses — choose only one that is truly yours:
- I haven't worked on very high-traffic systems yet, so I'm studying system design.
- I sometimes go deep into a problem alone before asking for help.
- I haven't used some tools in production yet (for example Next.js), so I'm building practice projects.
- I find it hard to say no to extra tasks, which can stretch my time.

## 🗣️ Sample answer

> "One weakness is that I sometimes try to solve a hard problem fully by myself before asking for help. [FILL IN: a small, real example — e.g. a time you spent too long on a bug.]
>
> I've realised that asking early is often faster for the team. So now I set myself a time limit. If I'm stuck for about [FILL IN: e.g. an hour], I write down what I've tried and ask a teammate. I also share progress in stand-ups earlier.
>
> It's helped. [FILL IN: a real improvement — e.g. blockers get solved faster.] I still like to try things first, but now I balance it better."

**Another option, if it's true for you:**

> "I haven't worked on very large-scale systems yet. Our platform is multi-tenant, but I want more experience with heavy traffic. So I'm studying system design — caching, queues, scaling — and practising designs out loud every week. [FILL IN: real progress.]"

## ✅ Do

- Pick a real weakness and own it calmly.
- Show a specific action you are taking.
- Mention real progress.
- Keep the weakness small and not core to the job.
- Practise saying it until it sounds natural.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't say "I'm a perfectionist" or "I work too hard".
- Don't say "I have no weaknesses".
- Don't name a core skill for the role as your weakness.
- Don't over-explain or sound worried.
- Don't share something personal or unrelated to work.

## 🔁 Follow-up questions

### Can you give another weakness?

Prepare a second one, also with an action. Keep it small. [FILL IN]

### How do you know this is improving?

Give evidence: feedback from a teammate, faster progress, a practice log. [FILL IN: your real evidence.]

### What feedback have you received from your manager?

[FILL IN: real feedback, and what you did with it.] Feedback you acted on is a strong answer.

### How would this weakness affect your work here?

Be honest and short: "Only a little, because I now [your action]." Then move on.
