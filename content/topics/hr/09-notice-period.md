## 💡 What they really want to know

HR asks this to plan:
- When can you start?
- Is the date fixed, or can it be shorter?
- Will you really join, or might you back out?

A clear, honest answer helps them, and helps you.

:::warning[Keep real details private]
Write your exact notice period and dates in your private notes, not on this public page.
:::

## 🏠 Real-life example

Think of a **class monitor changing schools**.

They don't just leave in the middle of term. They tell the teacher early, finish the term, and hand the class register to the next monitor.

- **Telling the teacher early** = resigning and serving notice.
- **Finishing the term** = your notice period.
- **Handing over the register** = a proper handover of your work.

## 🧩 How to structure your answer

1. **State your notice period** clearly.
2. **Mention any flexibility:** early release or buyout, only if it's realistic.
3. **Show responsibility:** you will do a clean handover.

**Common terms:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Notice period** | Days you must work after resigning (from your contract). |
| **Serving notice** | Working those days normally. |
| **Early release** | Your company lets you leave earlier, often after handover. |
| **Buyout** | Paying for the days you don't serve, sometimes covered by the new employer. |
| **Last working day (LWD)** | Your final day at the current company. |

## ✅ Do

- Check your contract or HR policy for the exact notice period.
- Give the real date, and update HR if it changes.
- Offer a clean handover plan.
- Ask the new company if they support a buyout, if you need one.
- Get your joining date confirmed in writing.

## ❌ Don't

- Don't give a shorter notice period than the real one.
- Don't promise an early release you haven't confirmed.
- Don't leave without a handover.
- Don't accept an offer you don't plan to join.
- Don't post exact dates on this public site.
